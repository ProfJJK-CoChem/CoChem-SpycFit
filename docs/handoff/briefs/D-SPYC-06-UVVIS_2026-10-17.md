### Question
Should SpycFit v2 fit UV-Vis spectra (electronic transitions), which your 2026-10-16 list (IR, Raman, THz, near-IR, microwave) leaves out, and if so, which kind?

### Why it matters now
- SRS v2 §1.2 "Is not" and the capability matrix cannot close (`docs/srs/CoChem-SpycFit_SRS_v2.md` at `fadb028`, l.143-174). UV-Vis is D-SPYC-06's last open item (§11, l.1457).
- A new matrix row needs a V-DEMO-5 demonstration, data and engine qualification (SF-SCOPE-002, l.626-629).
- BASE Manual Ch. 5 offers `Type = ... UVVIS` (`CoChem-BASE/CoChem_User_Manual.md` l.854); its revision waits on this (`docs/handoff/REVIEW_ERRATA.md` l.44-47).

### Context
- Your item-20 reply (2026-10-17): "I think the calculations and requirements for fitting UV-Vis share little to no overlap with rotovibrational spectroscopy predictions" (`docs/decisions/D-SPYC-06.md` l.23-26).
- Agent assessment: the view holds for broad and condensed-phase bands, which need TD-DFT, Franck-Condon/Herzberg-Teller (FC/HT) shapes and solvent models unused in P1-P14. It holds only in part for rotationally resolved electronic spectra: both states use a rigid-rotor Hamiltonian; only the band origin, transition-moment orientation and LIF intensities are new (Meerts et al. 2004, §3.1, doi:10.1139/V04-037).
- SRS v2 reads your "excited-state" extension (`docs/HANDOFF.md` l.29) as vibrationally excited states (P3, l.159). A assumes this; if you meant electronic states, choose B or C.
- Method Matrix §2.2 lists no UV-Vis exclusion, but its last bullet confines the ecosystem to "rotational constants in the microwave regime" (`CoChem-BASE/Method_Matrix.md` l.257). The superseded 2026-08-07 plan quoted it to reject UV-Vis (`docs/legacy/2026-08-07_spycfit_architectural_changes.md` l.55). Your scope already exceeds it for IR and Raman (HANDOFF l.20), so it cannot settle UV-Vis alone.
- No module owns electronic predictions. TORQ D12 requires "explicit ownership" (`CoChem-TORQ/docs/wiki/review/Decisions_and_Risks.md` l.133); WP12 lists selected UV-visible interfaces or handoffs (`CoChem-TORQ_Implementation_SRS.md` l.505). TOPOS names TD-DFT only as an ORCA feature (`CoChem_TOPOS_Baseline_SRS.md` l.700).
- Region is not transition type. Ground-state water lines reach the UV, reliable to 37 000 cm⁻¹ (Conway et al. 2020, doi:10.5194/acp-20-10015-2020). HITRAN2024 holds O₂ electronic bands at 1.27 µm, 1.91 µm and 762 nm (Adkins et al. 2025, doi:10.1016/j.jqsrt.2025.109629). SF-SCOPE-003a keeps region and transition model apart (l.630-633).
- **Does the 2026-10-11 recommendation still fit?** In part. "None in SpycFit" (`docs/handoff/PI_DECISIONS.md` l.163) assumed the rotational-only scope you replaced, and its MM reason no longer settles it. Your stated view gives a new reason to exclude.

### Options
**A. Exclude electronic transitions: list them under §1.2 "Is not" and refuse them with a typed out-of-scope result.**
- Pros: Matches your list and view. Needs no owner or new row.
- Cons: Closes off rotationally resolved electronic spectra of jet-cooled complexes. Near-IR O₂ electronic bands in HITRAN imports are refused unless excepted. Ch. 5 `UVVIS` is a documentation mismatch (no code in `CoChem-BASE/src` at `7d5b8ac` parses `%spycfit`).
- Risks: The refusal needs a requirement and V- test, modelled on SF-MODEL-002b (l.648-650) and review negative control 13 (`docs/wiki/review/CoChem-SpycFit_SRS_Review.md` l.603). Where UV-Vis lives in CoChem stays open (TORQ D12/T10).

**B. A later profile, P15: rotationally resolved gas-phase electronic spectra, after P3 and P6; broad bands stay out.**
- Pros: Reuses P1 rotor models and P3/P6 multi-state machinery. Yields excited-state rotational constants GEOM could use (D-SPYC-10; l.145-147).
- Cons: PGOPHER, only proposed in the draft matrix (l.158-163), gets no further updates (notice of 23 Mar 2022, https://pgopher.chm.bris.ac.uk/); this also affects P2, P3, P6, P7. JB95 is a Windows executable (https://www.nist.gov/services-resources/software/jb95-spectral-fitting-program); Linux use UNVERIFIED. LIF intensities need a new SF-INT-001a kind (l.651-657). Owner: TORQ (WP12) or another module, by agreement.
- Risks: Dense spectra are fitted unassigned (Meerts abstract), so SF-HITL-002a actions (l.774-776) would act on whole bands. Triplet or open-shell states need spin terms (MM l.254 excludes radicals); closed-shell cases like Meerts' do not.

**C. B plus condensed-phase electronic bands and vibronic envelopes, as Ch. 5 implies.**
- Pros: Serves the Manual and MM Table 10's teaching tier (l.3278).
- Cons: Shares only generic band shapes with P8. Predicted shapes need FC/HT providers (ORCA 6.1 ESD, harmonic, https://www.faccts.de/docs/orca/6.1/manual/contents/spectroscopyproperties/esd.html) and a new provider contract. Gas-phase anchors (HANDOFF l.23) exist mainly for small atmospheric molecules and radicals (Keller-Rudek et al. 2013, doi:10.5194/essd-5-365-2013).
- Risks: TD-DFT at ±0.2-0.3 eV (MM l.3286) constrains fits little. Departs most from your list and view.

**D. Empirical UV-Vis peak or envelope fitting only, with no electronic model.**
- Pros: Cheap; reuses P8 code.
- Cons: Band parameters only. Needs its own matrix row.
- Risks: Filing it under P8 would label electronic bands vibrational.

### Agent recommendation
Option A, worded by transition type ("electronic transitions"), not wavelength. It follows your list and view and needs no excited-state owner. Two optional conditions: (1) reserve an electronic-state field in state identity (SF-CMP-004a, l.1003) so B needs no data migration; (2) say whether near-IR O₂ electronic bands are excluded or excepted; excluded lines are labelled and refused, never silently dropped. Visible and near-UV overtones are vibrational, not electronic; extending P12 past the near-IR is a separate question.

Main risk: losing rotationally resolved electronic spectra of complexes; record B as a candidate amendment to reduce it. The refusal should name its SRS requirement id. D is not recommended.

### How to answer
